## Question No. 01.

**(a)** What is a conceptual framework? Why is a conceptual framework necessary in financial accounting?

**(b)** Expenses, losses, and distributions to owners are all decreases in net assets. What are the distinctions among them?

**(c)** The life of a business is divided into specific time periods, usually a year, to measure results of operations for each such time period and to portray financial conditions at the end of each period. What has been the effect of this practice on accounting? What is its relation to the accrual system? What influence has it had on accounting entries?

**(d)** The chairman of the board of directors of the company for which you are chief accountant has told you that he has little use for accounting figures based on historical cost. He believes that replacement values or fair values are of far more significance to the board of directors than "out-of-date costs." Present some arguments to convince him that accounting data should still be based on historical cost.

Marks: [3+3+6+3] = [15]

## Question No. 02.

**(a)** Martin Buber Co. purchased land as a factory site for $400,000. The process of tearing down two old buildings on the site and constructing the factory required 6 months. The company paid $42,000 to raze the old buildings and sold salvaged lumber and brick for $6,300. Legal fees of $1,850 were paid for title investigation and drawing the purchase contract. Martin Buber paid $2,200 to an engineering firm for a land survey, and $68,000 for drawing the factory plans. The land survey had to be made before definitive plans could be drawn. Title insurance on the property cost $1,500, and a liability insurance premium paid during construction was $900. The contractor's charge for construction was $2,740,000. The company paid the contractor in two instalments: $1,200,000 at the end of 3 months and $1,540,000 upon completion. Determine the cost of the land and the cost of the building as they should be recorded on the books of Martin Buber Co. Assume that the land survey was for the building.

**(b)** Identify the factors that are relevant in determining the annual depreciation charge, and explain whether these factors are determined objectively or whether they are based on judgment.

**(c)** The cost of equipment purchased by Charleston, Inc., on June 1, 2014, is $89,000. It is estimated that the machine will have a $5,000 salvage value at the end of its service life. Its service life is estimated at 7 years, its total working hours are estimated at 42,000, and its total production is estimated at 525,000 units. During 2014, the machine was operated 6,000 hours and produced 55,000 units. During 2015, the machine was operated 5,500 hours and produced 48,000 units.

Required:
Compute depreciation expense on the machine for the year ending December 31, 2014, and the year ending December 31, 2015, using the following methods. (a) Straight-line. (b) Units-of-output. (c) Declining-balance (twice the straight-line rate).

Marks: [5+5+5] = [15]

## Question No. 03.

**(a)** Differentiate between cost model and revaluation model in recognition and valuation of property, plant and equipment.

**(b)** What criteria are met to pass the impairment test?

**(c)** AB Limited operates in the pharmaceutical business. The following information relates to the company's activities in research and development for the year ended on 31 October 2020.

1. Commercial production started on 1 June 2016 for Formula A. By 31 October 2019 Tk. 43,000 had been capitalized in respect of development expenditure on this product. During the year a further Tk. 10,000 was spent on development of this product.
AB Limited has taken out a patent in respect of Formula A which will last for ten years. Legal and administrative expenses in relation to this of Tk. 2,000 were incurred on 1 November 2019. In the current year, sales of Formula A amounted to Tk. 50,000. Sales are expected to be made over the next three years of Tk. 150,000, Tk. 200,000 and Tk. 100,000 respectively.

2. The development of Formula B is at an earlier stage. Although the company believes it has a reasonable expectation of future benefits from this project it has not yet been able to demonstrate this with sufficient certainty. Expenditure on this project in the current year was Tk. 20,000.

Requirements:

**i.** Calculate the total amount to be recognized in profit or loss in respect of the above in the year ended on 31 October 2020.

**ii.** Draft the table showing the movement on intangible assets which should appear in the notes to the Financial Statements of AB Limited for the year ended 31 October 2020.

Marks: [2.5+2.5+(4+6)] = [15]

## Question No. 04.

**(a)** Identify and explain the three types of classifications for investments in debt securities.

**(b)** What are hybrid securities? Give an example of a hybrid security.

**(c)** The following information relates to the classification as held for sale of two machines by Halwell Ltd.

| | Machine A | Machine B |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | BDT 120000 | BDT 100000 |
| Fair value less costs to sell | 90000 | 40000 |
| Anticipated gain/ (loss) on sale (based on fair value) | 30000 | (20000) |

In accordance with IFRS 5 *Non-current Assets Held for Sale and Discontinued Operations* what was total accumulated depreciation on machines classified as held for sale?

**(d)** The following information about plant and equipment was disclosed in the financial statements of Maine Ltd. for the year ended 31 December 2022.

| | 2022 | 2021 |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | 735000 | 576000 |
| Accumulated depreciation | (265000) | (315000) |
| Carrying amount | 470000 | 261000 |

During 2022 expenditure on plant and equipment was incurred amounted to BDT 512000. Impairment loss on reclassification of old plant as held for sale was BDT 50000. Loss on the disposal of old plant amounted to BDT 57000. Depreciation charge on plant and equipment was BDT 143000.

Determine the amount of sales proceeds received on the disposal of the old plant as per IFRS 5 *Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations*.

## Question No. 05.

**(a)** Of what merit is the contention that the allowance method lacks the objectivity of the direct write-off? Discuss in terms of accounting's measurement function.

**(b)** On January 1, 2020, Ha-meem Group sells property for which it had paid $690,000 to Beximco Textile Division Limited, receiving in return Beximco Textile's zero-interest-bearing note for $1,000,000 payable in 5 years. What entry would Ha-meem Group make to record the sale, assuming that Ha-meem Group frequently sells similar items of property for a cash sales price of $640,000?

**(c)** Indicate three reasons why a company might sell its receivables to another company.

**(d)** The chief accountant for Square Textile provides you with the following list of accounts receivable written off in the current year.

| Date | Customer | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| March 31 | Opex Sinha Group | $7,800 |
| June 30 | Thermax Group | 6,700 |
| September 30 | DBL Group | 7,000 |
| December 31 | Viyellatex Group | 9,830 |

Square Textile follows the policy of debiting Bad Debt Expense as accounts are written off. The chief accountant maintains that this procedure is appropriate for financial statement purposes because the Internal Revenue Service will not accept other methods for recognizing bad debts.

All of Square Textile's sales are on a 30-day credit basis. Sales for the current year total $2,200,000, and research has determined that bad debt losses approximate 2% of sales.

Required:

**i)** Do you agree or disagree with Square Textile's policy concerning recognition of bad debt expense? Why or why not?

**ii)** By what amount would net income differ if bad debt expense was computed using the percentage-of-sales approach?

Marks: [3+3+2+7] = [15]

## Question No. 06.

**(a)** Identify the two recognized lease accounting methods for lessees and distinguish between them.

**(b)** Distinguish between minimum rental payments and minimum lease payments, and indicate what is included in minimum lease payment.

**(c)** Explain the distinction between a direct-financing lease and a sales-type lease for a lessor.

**(d)** Bangladesh Machine Tools Factory, a machinery dealer, leased a machine to Bangladesh Petroleum Corporation on January 1, 2020. The lease is for an 8-year period and requires equal annual payments of $35,013 at the beginning of each year. The first payment is received on January 1, 2020. Bangladesh Machine Tools Factory had purchased the machine during 2019 for $160,000. Collectability of lease payments is reasonably predictable, and no important uncertainties surround the amount of costs yet to be incurred by Bangladesh Machine Tools Factory. Bangladesh Machine Tools Factory set the annual rental to ensure an 11% rate of return. The machine has an economic life of 10 years with no residual value and reverts to Bangladesh Machine Tools Factory at the termination of the lease.

Required:

**i)** Compute the amount of the lease receivable.

**ii)** Prepare all the necessary journal entries for Bangladesh Machine Tools Factory for 2020.
