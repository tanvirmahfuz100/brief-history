**c)** With more than 3,500 locations in 44 states, Sonic is the largest chain of drive-in fast-food restaurants in the United States. Unlike other chains, many of Sonic's locations are individually owned and operated. That's because Sonic knows the value of motivated entrepreneurs who are willing to put everything they've got into their businesses. Sonic has stuck to this go-getter attitude thanks to the leadership of founder Troy Smith. After coming back from World War II, Smith took a job as a milkman until he realized that he wanted to run a business of his own. Over the next few years he opened several restaurants in his hometown of Shawnee, Oklahoma, ranging from a diner to his own fried chicken chain to see what worked best for him. Since he ran these businesses by himself as sole proprietorships, this meant he could make all the major decisions by himself, but he had no one to help him make the restaurants great. As a result, most of Smith's early ventures failed. He didn't let those initial disappointments set him back, however. Eventually Smith bought a five acre gravel lot that contained a little root beer stand and a big log cabin. While he planned to turn the latter into a steakhouse, Smith nearly tore down the root beer stand to make room for more parking. As time went by, though, he realized that the soda stand was actually doing better business than the steakhouse. After a few years, a larger place in town burnt down, Smith thought that was just the thing; his small soda stand had the potential to become a big success. He constructed a speaker system to deliver a menu to the parking lot and hired carhops to bring food directly to customers' cars. Soon enough, revenue at the Top Hat Drive-In had exploded.
