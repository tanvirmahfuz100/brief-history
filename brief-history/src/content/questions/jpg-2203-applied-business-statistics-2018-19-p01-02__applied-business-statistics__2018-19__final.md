## Question 2 — [3+4+4+4]
**(a)** Explain the characteristics of the normal probability distribution. [03]
**(b)** Define standard error. Explain the significances of standard error in decision making. [04]
**(c)** The manager of a spiffy Lube auto lubrication shop is trying to reverse his policy on ordering grease gun cartridges. Currently, he orders 110 cartridges per week; but he runs out of cartridges 1 out of every 4 weeks. He knows that, on average, the shop uses 95 cartridges per week. He is also willing to assume that demand for cartridges is normally distributed. [04]
**i.** What is the standard deviation of this distribution?
**ii.** If manager wants to order enough cartridges so that his probability of running out during any week is no greater than 0.2, how many cartridge should he order per week?
**(d)** Harries Polls, Inc. is in the business of surveying households. Form previous surveys, it is known that the standard deviation of the number of hours of television watched in a week by a household is 1.1 hours. Harries Polls would like to determine the average number of hours of television watched per week per household in the U.S. Accuracy is important, so Harries Polls would like to be 98 percent certain that the sample average number of hours fall within ±0.3 hour of the national average. Conservatively, what sample size should Harries Polls use? [04]

## Question 3 — [5+5+5]
**(a)** Define degree of freedom. Differentiate between point estimates and interval estimate. [05]
**(b)** A company recently criticized for not paying women as much as men working in the same positions. claims that its average salary paid to all employees is $23,500. From a random sample of 29 women in the company, the average salary was calculated to be $23,000. If the population standard deviation is known to be $1250 for these jobs, determine whether we could reasonably (within 2 standard errors) expect to find $23,000 as the sample mean if, in fact, the company's claim is true. [05]
**(c)** A personnel manager believed that 18% of the company's employees work overtime every week. If the observed proportion this week is 13% in a sample of 250 of the 2,500 employees, can we accept her belief as reasonable or must we conduct that some other value is more appropriate? Use $\alpha = 0.05$. [05]
